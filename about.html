About Civic Sparrow

Making civics clear, calm and welcoming

We believe everyone deserves to understand how their government works — without jargon, spin or feeling talked down to.

Our story

Why Civic Sparrow exists

Ask a group of adults how a bill becomes law, what the Tenth Amendment says or who runs their local school board, and many will hesitate. That's not a lack of interest. Civics is often taught once, quickly, and then rarely revisited — and much of what people hear afterwards comes wrapped in argument.

Civic Sparrow was created to be the calm, reliable explainer in the middle of all that noise. We focus on the structure and processes of government, the founding documents and the practical ways people take part in public life. We leave the conclusions to our readers.

The sparrow is our reminder of who civics is really for. Sparrows aren't rare or grand. They're everywhere, living in every kind of neighborhood — much like the everyday people whose participation keeps a democracy working.

What we stand for

Our three commitments

01

Nonpartisan, always

We describe how government works and what documents say. We don't endorse candidates, parties or policies, and where reasonable people disagree, we present the main views fairly.

02

Grounded in sources

We build lessons from primary sources such as the Constitution and official government information, and we correct errors quickly and openly.

03

Written for everyone

Our writing is original, plain and accessible, so a curious twelve-year-old and a busy parent can learn side by side.

How we create lessons

From primary source to plain English

Every unit starts with the original text or an official source. We read it closely, identify the ideas a learner most needs, and then explain them using everyday examples. Drafts are checked against the source again before publishing, and our interactive tools — the quiz, Bill of Rights explorer and glossary — are reviewed the same way.

We write every lesson ourselves. We don't copy text from textbooks or other websites, and the photographs on our site are used under the free Pexels license. You can read the details in our Editorial Policy.

See how we work with schools

Who we serve

Our lessons are designed for middle and high school students, their teachers and families, and adults who want a clear refresher — including community groups, libraries and adult learners. Everything on our website is free to read and use for personal and classroom learning.

Accessibility

We aim to make every page usable by as many people as possible, with clear headings, readable type, keyboard-friendly tools and descriptive image text. If something doesn't work for you, please see our Accessibility Statement and let us know.
